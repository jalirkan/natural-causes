# Direction panel — 2026-09-27

Raw output of five independent design agents, each given one angle on the
same brief (the game's spirit as Justin stated it after playing: a roguelite,
fun and varied upgrades, tongue-in-cheek about life, cartoonish; art direction
open; D-007 absolute). Unjudged: the judging stage was cut when work moved off
the cloud. Read it as four opinions to steal from, not a decision. The
synthesis and the G-038 batch retirement are the next session's job (HANDOFF.md).

## The Whole Life Is One Run  
*angle: propose:genre-purist*

**Thesis.** Natural Causes should be a real Vampire Survivors run — a thirty-minute climb from one weak shot to a screen that dies on contact — where the seven acts are the run's minute-markers, the items you took as a sperm are still what you do at forty, and every boss is a deadline rather than a monster. The life theme is not a cost to the loop; it is the best excuse the genre has ever had for persistence, evolutions, meta-progression and a death screen that says why, and the current design keeps refusing the gifts (every upgrade a downgrade, thirty items for seven acts, a five-minute self-contained act, a boss that holds still) that the theme is trying to hand it.

**The run.** **One run is one life: about 30 minutes, continuous, seven acts of roughly four minutes each, items and levels carried the whole way.** Ship three acts first (Conception, School, The Office — about 13 minutes) with a stated roadmap; the run ends at whatever the last shipped act is until Decline exists. This is the genre's proven arc — VS is a 30-minute clock with a Reaper at the end — and the premise IS that clock. Nobody has to invent the structure; the life already has minute-markers.

**Acts are costume changes at a deadline, not restarts.** At an act's end the field stops spawning, the boss appears, and the act resolves on a *deadline* (see the Egg below). On success the screen wipes to the next act: same player sprite, same cowlick, same items, new background, new horde in the new costume. The five sim verbs the enemies are built from (chase / cross / drift / static, damage / engulf / attach / none, bounce / patrol / merge / burst) mean every act re-costumes the same five pressure slots — the chaser swarm, the crosser, the zone, the merger, the unkillable attacher — which is G-001 literally implemented and cheaper than designing seven unrelated rosters.

**The power curve is the whole game.** Minute 1: one seeking bolt. Minute 8: three weapons, two of them evolved, the screen is a firework. Minute 25: you are standing still and the horde is dying at the edge of the screen. The XP curve is VS-shaped (cheap early levels, a level every 20–30 seconds by act two, slowing to one a minute by Family) instead of the current `5 + 4.5·level`, which the bots exhaust — membrane+acrosome reaches level 22 of a 35-level pool in five minutes, and a pool that runs out is a run that stops being a run.

**The antibody stays and becomes the spine's counter-pressure** (ITEMS-BRIEF §3.3, answered yes). Every act has one unkillable attacher in its costume — antibody, permanent record, performance review, HOA letter, your knees — and stacks persist for the run. The genre's answer to a persistent debuff is a build answer, not a reset: there is exactly one item that touches stacks (Therapy, below), and it costs a slot for life. What the stacks do to the run is honest and funny: the kiting build you made at School is, by Decline, a turret, because you cannot run any more. That is also exactly what late-game VS looks like.

**Winning:** you reach the end of Decline and die of natural causes. Certificate (G-002). **Losing:** you die early, and the death screen names it — *Cause of death: dodgeball. Age 9.* / *Cause of death: reply-all. Age 34.* — from the enemy that landed the last hit and the act clock. Every loss is a small obituary, and it is the run-to-run hook in one line of copy.

**What persists between runs — The Estate.** Meta-progression is heredity. XP earned in a run converts at death to *estate*, spent between runs on permanent starting bonuses (the VS PowerUp board — Might, Armour, Luck, one extra starting HP tier — with the copy "You inherited: slightly better knees"). Unlocks by achievement: new *temperaments* (the VS character roster — The Anxious One starts with Small Talk at level 2 and 80% max HP; The Late Bloomer starts with Capacitation; The Big Baby starts with +30% HP and −15% speed), new weapons ("Survive School without taking damage: unlocks Homeschooled"), and the fourth offer button, **Decline** ("You may decline. You have never once done that.") — VS's skip, unlocked, not default. The Estate lives entirely outside `World` (a `src/meta/` module read by `ActScene` and passed in via `WorldOptions`), so the bots run an inheritance-free life unless told otherwise and the sim stays pure. Inheritance (G-017) is built now, at the Egg, because a run that spans acts is the thing it was waiting for.

**Upgrades.** **The system, stated in VS terms because that is what makes it compulsive:** six weapon slots, six passive slots, weapons level 1–8, passives 1–5, and a weapon at max level evolves when the player holds its paired passive — the recipe is hinted on the card ("Evolves with: Thick Skin") the first time and remembered forever after. **Most items are pure gains.** G-014 is retired as a universal rule; the *trade* becomes a small, named category (life-bargains — three or four items whose blurb is a deal) so that "this one costs something" is a flavour a player notices rather than the water they swim in. A level-up must be a climb, not a coin flip: the two-second card decision only works when every card is good and the question is *which good thing*.

**Budget:** about 40 for the whole run, all act-agnostic — 12 weapons, 10 passives, 12 evolutions, plus the 3 inheritances and 3–5 temperaments. Retires "roughly thirty, seven per act". Persistence is what makes 40 enough: a run of 30 minutes with 40 items is dense; seven runs of five minutes with 30 items shared out is thin, which is what Justin played.

**Naming:** items are named for the *behaviour* in life vocabulary, so the same item is legible from Conception to Decline (ITEMS-BRIEF §3.2, option two). The seven existing items keep their mechanics and lose their sperm names: Lash → Small Talk, Motility → Ambition, Acrosome → Coping Mechanism, Wake → Baggage, Chemotaxis → Charisma, Midpiece → Youth, Membrane → Thick Skin, Capacitation stays Capacitation (the one biological word that is also a joke about a 40-year-old). G-035/G-036 hold: the card's object is the field effect, and the object does not change costume per act — a paper dart thrown by a sperm and a paper dart thrown in a meeting is the joke.

**The passive vector is the genre's, in the life's words:** Ambition (Might), Reach (Area), Restlessness (Cooldown), Stamina (Duration), Connections (Amount), Appetite (magnet radius), Thick Skin (armour), Constitution (max HP), Youth (speed), Born Lucky (luck). All pure gains except the two life-bargains below.

**What makes two builds feel different:** slots and evolution pairs, not penalties. A Grudge / Coping Mechanism / Thick Skin / Stamina build stands in the crowd and grinds; a Baggage / Ambition / Youth / Restlessness build never stops moving; a Small Talk / Connections / Reach build clears from range and hopes. Then the antibody spine bends all three the same direction across the run, and the interesting question becomes *when* your build has to become a turret, not whether.

**Five items that could ship:**

1. **Grudge** (weapon, orbit — new mode, VS's King Bible). *"Three things you are still mad about circle you at all times."* Three orbiting objects at level 1, +1 per two levels, damage on contact. Evolves with Stamina into **The Thing You Said In 2009**: the orbit is permanent, never despawns, and grows a fourth object every act.
2. **Coping Mechanism** (weapon, burst — Acrosome renamed). *"Hurts everything you touch. Everyone says you should talk to someone."* Burst around the player on a cooldown; levels add radius. Evolves with Thick Skin into **Personality**: the burst becomes a constant damaging aura you cannot turn off.
3. **Connections** (passive, Amount). *"One more of everything. You know a guy."* +1 projectile / orbit / footprint on every weapon per level. The multiplicative item that makes minute 12 look like minute 12.
4. **Therapy** (passive, life-bargain — the one G-014 item that stays). *"Removes one thing from your file every ten seconds. You will be doing this for the rest of your life."* Sheds one antibody-family stack per 10s; occupies a passive slot permanently and cannot be evolved away. It is the only counter to the spine, it costs a sixth of your build, and the bots can measure exactly what a slot is worth.
5. **Inheritance: Sensitivity** (assigned at the Egg, not chosen — G-017 built). *"You feel everything. It turns out that is a pickup radius."* Much larger magnet radius; +25% contact damage taken. The first item in the run is the one you did not pick.

A sixth for the record, because the joke is the mechanic: **Homeschooled** (unlockable weapon — line, fires backward). *"Very strong in one direction nobody else is facing."*

**Enemies and bosses.** **Philosophy:** an act is five pressure slots re-costumed, and every enemy is a condition, institution or abstraction (D-007, absolute). The sim already speaks the verbs; new acts should mostly be data, and the roster should add a sim verb only when a costume genuinely needs one. The genre rule I would hold every enemy to: *a horde enemy is a way of being killed the player can name in one word* — chaser, wall, zone, ball, file. The theme's rule is law 8: none of it is paying attention to you. Those two rules agree, which is why this game can be funny at 60 spawns a minute without a line of dialogue.

**Three enemies that could ship:**

1. **Reply-All** (The Office, chase, contact damage — one new verb: *split*). A slow chaser; when killed it spawns two smaller "Re:" chasers, which spawn two "Re: Re:" each, three generations deep, all with a face. The joke is mechanical: doing something about it is what multiplies it, and a screen-clearing build turns the Office into a blizzard of its own success. `whyThisStage`: "The Office is the first place where dealing with a thing is precisely what makes more of it."
2. **Group Project** (College, drift + merge — the verb homework already has, applied to a mover). Spawns in fours; any two that touch fuse into one with the summed HP and one face doing all the work; the merged body keeps drifting. It drops XP once, on the last hit. `whyThisStage`: "College is where the player is first graded on something five people were assigned and one person did."
3. **Your Knees** (Decline, drift, attach, invulnerable, spawns at `lead`). The antibody's final costume, mechanically identical: unkillable, costs speed and never health, already where you are going. It is drawn as two knees with one worried face between them. By this act the stacks the player has carried since Conception are the real boss, and Your Knees is just the last clerk to add to the file. `whyThisStage`: "Decline is where the record the player has been accumulating since before they were a person is finally read back to them by their own body."

**One boss that does something — The Egg, re-fought as a race.** Keep G-006 (absorption, not a kill) and keep the Egg stationary and serene; retire G-019's "does nothing". At 300s the field stops spawning and the Egg appears — and every rival sperm still alive (the bots have 700–1,200 of them on the field at that moment) turns and swims for it, using the existing `chase` verb with the boss as the target. Each rival that reaches the corona is absorbed and fills a **someone-else meter**; the player must land BOSS_HP of damage before it fills. The Egg's five-shot telegraphed spread stays (G-010: the first aimed thing in your life is the thing deciding whether you exist) and now it also thins the rivals, so standing in its fire is a strategy. The joke lands on the loss screen: *"The Egg chose someone. You were close."* The boss does not move, does not enrage, does not phase — it is a *deadline with a crowd*, which is what every boss in this game should be: the Gym Teacher picks teams and you are the last one standing on the line; the Reorg (G-004, keep as written) rearranges; Time, in Decline, is a clock you cannot damage and the win is that it runs out.

**Art register.** **Picture-book sticker.** The visual language of the first book everyone got — a condition is a shape with a face — printed as a vinyl sticker sheet: thick uniform outline, fully saturated flat fills, big deadpan two-dots-and-a-line faces, and a **die-cut white rim** around every sprite, the way a sticker has a margin of backing around it. Reference points: Mr. Men / Little Miss for the vocabulary (Mr. Tickle and Little Miss Bossy are already law 9 — a role with a face, never a person), Brotato for the proof that flat, ultra-simple, funny shapes carry a horde at tiny size on a phone, 1990s sticker sheets and Trapper Keepers for the print artefact. The joke: the life is told in the register it started in — Mr. Sperm, Little Miss Dodgeball, Mr. Reply-All, Mr. Mortgage — and the register never grows up, because the player never does (G-003). The single exception is the certificate at the end, which stays mid-century institutional: your life in a sticker book, your death in a benefits pamphlet. That is the one place the old register's joke actually lands, and it is a page the camera rests on.

**Why it reads at 48px in a horde**, which the current register does not (D-018 says so; the sprites say so — the player, the rival and the Egg are three beige shapes on off-white): (1) saturated fill against a mid-value act ground is the highest-contrast combination flat art can make; (2) the die-cut rim is a guaranteed 2px light edge on every silhouette against any act tone — it is the halo VS pixel art gets from its dark outline on a dark field, done as a print artefact, so "printed, not rendered" survives; (3) the faces are already drawn at sticker scale — at 48px a Mr. Men face is still a face, and law 5 (faces on everything) is where the comedy actually lives on screen, so the register should be the one that makes faces readable rather than the one that makes them a pale smudge.

**What it does to the laws.** Laws 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 stand unchanged; the pipeline's six stages stand. Law 3 is rewritten: the palette stays locked and enforced, but it is a saturated set of ~24 plus a reserved `vinyl` white for the rim (law 10 gains a row: vinyl belongs to the die-cut and to nothing else; paper stays the player's). D-018's detail budget is retired — there is no halftone or grain anywhere but the certificate — which removes the register/genre conflict D-018 was managing instead of resolving. CONFORM gains one step (the rim); CHECK gains one criterion (rim present, closed, correct colour). G-008's rationale ("the pamphlet is a specimen of the institution") is honest and was invisible at gameplay size; it moves to the certificate, where it is visible. **Nothing is law until the test batch** (PLAN.md): six sprites, two acts, on both backgrounds, at 48px, at the link, judged by Justin — the same gate that promoted the last register.

**Retire.**
- `G-014 (every Conception item subtracts something)` — It fights the genre's engine. A level-up is the ten-second compulsive decision of a survivors run only when every card is a gain and the question is which gain; when every card is a trade the decision becomes a coin flip and the run never climbs. The bots show the cost: 13–50% win rates and a power curve that plateaus. Keep the trade as a small named category (life-bargains: Therapy, Sensitivity) so 'this one costs something' is a flavour rather than the water.
- `PLAN.md mechanism 5's 30-item cap and CONCEPTION-ROSTER §4's 'seven is Conception's full allocation'` — Thirty across seven acts is four per act, which is not a build. Forty for one continuous 30-minute run, all persistent and act-agnostic, is dense. The quality gate (each item names what build it enables) stays; the number and the per-act split go.
- `G-019 (the Egg holds still and does nothing)` — A stationary shooting gallery is a boss the genre has no use for — four of five bot policies leave it at 0%. Keep G-006's absorption and keep the Egg still; make the fight a race the rivals are also running. The boss becomes a deadline with a crowd, which is what every boss here should be.
- `README/acts.ts: an act is a self-contained five-minute run with items reset` — A five-minute run is what Justin played and it is why there was 'obviously a lot to do': the genre's arc needs 20–30 minutes to climb. The seven acts are the minute-markers of one life; items, levels and stacks carry across them.
- `D-017 / G-008 / ART-DIRECTION 'mid-century institutional' register, and D-018's detail budget` — D-018 admits the register is built from fine line and halftone that are illegible below ~100px, and every sprite in the game is 44–96px moving at speed; what shipped is beige on off-white. The pamphlet joke lands on the certificate, where the camera rests, and nowhere else. Replace with the picture-book sticker register; keep laws 1–2 and 4–11 and the whole pipeline; rewrite law 3's palette. Gate on a test batch, as before.
- `ITEMS-BRIEF §3.2's in-fiction-per-act item names (Acrosome, Motility, Midpiece)` — They cannot survive a run that spans acts — 'Acrosome' does not survive a performance review. Items take life-vocabulary names (Small Talk, Ambition, Coping Mechanism, Baggage, Charisma, Youth, Thick Skin); Conception's enemies keep their biological names per G-009.
- `G-017's 'do not build this yet' clause` — It was gated on a second act to carry the inheritance into. A continuous run is that act. Build the three inheritances at the Egg now.
- `world.ts xpToNext = 5 + 4.5·level and the 5-level cap on every item` — The bots reach level 22 of a 35-level pool in five minutes; a 30-minute run exhausts it in act two. Weapons go to level 8, passives to 5, evolutions on top, and the curve is VS-shaped: fast early, a level every 20–30s by School, one a minute by Family.

**Risks.**
- **Performance at a real power curve.** A VS-shaped climb means thousands of kills a minute and ~1,500 live bodies; the bots already sit at 700–1,200 alive at the Egg, `MAX_ACTIVE_ENEMIES` is 1,500, and the link has to run on a phone in Phaser. Mitigation is the existing pool (`systems/pool.ts`), a hard live cap that converts overflow into bigger/tankier spawns the way VS does, and an fps column in the bot report so the sim's cost is measured before the presentation's is.
- **Additive items converge on one build.** G-014's real argument was that pure gains produce runs that differ in magnitude rather than kind. The answer is structural — six-plus-six slots, evolutions that require specific pairs, the antibody spine bending every build the same way at a different time — and it is a claim the bots can check (uptake spread, win rate by build) and a human has to confirm. If the uptake table collapses to one build after build one, the fix is slots and recipes, not reintroducing penalties.
- **A second register change in the project's life.** The pamphlet joke was the best idea on paper the project has had and it was invisible at 48px; the sticker register could read as generic in exactly the way the first batch read as 'too toony'. That is why build three is a side-by-side test batch at gameplay size behind a flag and not a rewrite of ART-DIRECTION.md — the register is promoted by Justin looking at six sprites, or it is not promoted.

## Loud Pictures, Dry Words  
*angle: propose:comedy-first*

**Thesis.** The joke of this game is recognition — "oh no, it's Homework" — and recognition only fires when the thing is named on screen, drawn big enough to have a face, and does the literal thing it did to you; today every joke lives in a design document, behind a deniable biology name, in a pamphlet register that is a beige smudge at 48px (look at the atlas: the substitute is a tan man, the white cell is a maroon blur), and a player laughs zero times in five minutes. So: loud flat cartoon pictures and dry institutional words — the card, the death certificate, the report card — where every upgrade is a habit you actually acquired at that age and keep for life, every enemy is a thing you actually fought behaving exactly as it did, and the game still never explains the theme; it just stops hiding the punchlines.

**The run.** ONE RUN IS ONE LIFE, 25–30 minutes, seven acts. The act clock shrinks as you age — Conception 5:00, School 4:30, Adolescence 4:00, Service/College 3:30, Office 3:30, Family 3:00, Decline 2:30 — because time goes faster the older you get, and the game never says so; the HUD clock is labelled by age ("age 0", "age 6–11", "age 34") instead of minutes. Each act ends in its boss; beating it issues the act's DOCUMENT (a report card, a yearbook page, a performance review, a mortgage statement, an obituary) built from real run stats, and the next act starts with your build intact. LOSING: die in any act and you get a death certificate with the cause filled in by whatever killed you. WINNING: Decline's boss is Time, which has no health bar, only a clock; survive it and you die anyway, and that is the certificate the title promised. PERSISTS WITHIN A RUN: the build (items carry the whole life and get their life-names at the School transition), the Permanent Record (the antibody's drag stacks, act-agnostic from School on — the same unkillable Y in a different costume each act: the file, the transcript, the credit report), and Grudges. PERSISTS ACROSS RUNS: only the ancestor wall and one inherited passive (see build 3). No currency, no shop — a life about a life does not have a meta-shop.

TEN THINGS THE PLAYER WOULD SEE, in order:
1. Title screen: under the sperm with the cowlick, six small portraits of previous runs, each a cowlick on a shape: "Your mother. Age 0. Cause of death: White cell." "Your grandfather. Age 9. Dodgeball." "Great-aunt. Age 41. Reply All."
2. First spawn of each enemy type: a small-caps toast for 1.5s, Survivors-style — "RIVAL SPERM". Later: "HOMEWORK", "GROUP CHAT", "11:00 SYNC", "STAIRS". A name is not narration; it is the punchline.
3. The level-up card, one line (G-034 stays): "Motility — Forward, harder. The only direction you believe in." Then the School transition re-deals every carried item as a card reading "Stubbornness (was Motility)". You discover the thing you picked as a sperm is your personality.
4. Act-one win stays "you were let in", set as an admissions letter: "ADMITTED. Please report to School."
5. School's boss blows a whistle and the screen says PICK TEAMS; the slowest half of the horde stops hurting you. You were picked last, so your team is whoever was left.
6. School's document, a report card: "Effort: B. Conduct: see me. Dodgeballs survived: 31. Homework outstanding: 14 piles." Every line is a real counter.
7. The Office HUD corner: "Unread: 47", climbing.
8. The death certificate: "CERTIFICATE OF DEATH · Age 34 · The Office, 2:10 · Cause of death: Reply All (thread of 62) · Contributing factors: Coffee 4, Grudge 2, Stubbornness 5 · R to be conceived again."
9. Family: a Toddler attached to your leg, delighted, while a Strongly Worded Letter lands five seconds late exactly where the problem used to be.
10. The win: the obituary. "Died peacefully of natural causes, aged 84, surrounded by Stubbornness 5, Thick Skin 4 and a Grudge. Predeceased by 41 previous attempts."

**Upgrades.** THE SYSTEM. Upgrades are HABITS, and habits persist: an item picked in Conception is still on you in Decline (ITEMS-BRIEF 3.1, middle option). Each item has a Conception name and a LIFE NAME it takes from School on, shown once on the transition card: Lash → Reflex, Motility → Stubbornness, Acrosome → Temper, Wake → Baggage ("everything behind you regrets it"), Chemotaxis → Charisma ("everything finds you attractive; this is not a compliment"), Midpiece → Restlessness, Membrane → Thick Skin, Capacitation → Late Bloomer. The existing blurbs already read as personality traits; the rename costs one field. BUDGET: ~50, not 30 — six TEMPERAMENTS (universal passives, in every act's pool), five or six HABITS per act (weapons and controls that only enter the pool in their act and then stay), and EVOLUTIONS. The mechanism-5 gate stays: `enables` is mandatory; `tradesAway` becomes a joke where a cost is funny, not a tax on every card (G-014 retired). The OFFER stays three cards, one line each; the per-act pool is temperaments + this act's habits + upgrades to what you already carry, so by the Office the cards are half your childhood and half your job. EVOLUTIONS happen to you: a habit at max plus a specific temperament turns into its adult form at the next level-up, dealt as a single card with no choice — "Principles (Stubbornness + Thick Skin): the line now also blocks projectiles for half a second" or "Tantrum (Temper + Restlessness): the burst knocks everything back". That is G-017's unchosen item, moved to where it is funny. WHAT MAKES TWO BUILDS DIFFER: temperament sets how you move (Restless kites, Thick Skin sits in it, Late Bloomer plays for the clock); habits stack across acts, so one player reaches the Office as a Stubborn lineman whose Reply-All splits along the column, another as a Charismatic chain-reactor whose Group Chat never stops; Grudge changes what you hunt, so two identical builds play differently by act three depending on who hit them.

FIVE ITEMS THAT COULD SHIP:
1. GROUP CHAT (Adolescence, weapon, mode `chain`). "Everyone hears about it." Damage dealt to any enemy spreads at 50% to every enemy within 160px of it, one hop per level (lv5 = five hops). Evolves with Charisma into SCREENSHOT: hops no longer lose damage.
2. REPLY ALL (Office, weapon, mode `split`). "One email. Forty recipients." A seeking shot that, on first hit, splits into one 1-damage shot per enemy within 200px. Cost: its cooldown grows 0.1s per recipient hit until the thread dies (ten seconds without a hit). The death certificate reports the thread length.
3. STRONGLY WORDED LETTER (Family, weapon, mode `delayed`). "It arrives in four to six seconds." Marks the nearest enemy's current position; five seconds later, 40 damage in a 120px circle at the mark. Lv5: three letters in flight. The problem has usually moved. Usually. ## The Classroom Reel — a register born at 48px  
*angle: propose:art-director*

**Thesis.** Keep G-008's one real insight — the game is drawn as an institutional artifact, not as a comment on one — and swap the artifact: from the 1950s benefits pamphlet, whose fine line and halftone the project has already admitted die below 100px (D-018), to the 1950s institutional cartoon — the civil-defence short, the classroom science reel that drew blood cells as characters for ten-year-olds — which was designed for a blurry 16mm projector and is cartoonish by birth. Everything in the eleven laws that exists for legibility stays and gets enforced harder; everything that exists for restraint goes, because restraint is the thing the owner played and did not enjoy.

**The run.** One run is one life, played straight through: every act back to back, no act select, about three and a half minutes of horde per act plus the boss, so a seven-act life is 25–30 minutes and a three-act life (Conception, School, Adolescence — the "three excellent acts" PLAN.md already prefers) is about twelve. The clock per act drops from 300s to ~210s: nothing new arrives after the act's midpoint anyway (acts.ts), and the owner's "went again?" question is answered faster by a shorter act with a boss in it than a longer one without.

Losing is dying in an act, and the game issues the same certificate it issues for winning, filled in honestly: the act, the age it implies, and the cause — the enemy that landed the last hit, by its in-act name. "Cause of death: Homework. Age 9." "Cause of death: A meeting. Age 41." Winning is the last act's boss, Time, and the certificate says Natural Causes (G-002, unchanged). The certificate is the end screen for both outcomes, so every run ends with the same form and only the one field differs; that is the whole joke and it is never explained.

Within a run, items persist across acts and are renamed per act — ITEMS-BRIEF §3.1's middle option and §3.2's third, settled together: the same seven Conception items carry into School wearing School's names on the cards (Lash → Reflex, Motility → Forward, Wake → Reputation, Chemotaxis → Popularity, Midpiece → Growth Spurt, Membrane → Thick Skin, Capacitation → Late Bloomer), mechanically identical, so the reflex you developed before you were a person is still what you do at forty — which is the spine (G-001) applied to the player instead of the horde. The Egg's inheritance (G-017) becomes buildable the moment School is startable. Each act adds two or three items of its own to the pool; nothing is removed.

Between runs, one thing persists: the certificate drawer — every death you have died, as a stack of forms on the title screen. No stat carries over (a life does not inherit its last life's health bar), but items unlock by cause of death: die to Spermicide once and Membrane is in the pool from the start of the next life; die to Homework and Snooze appears. Unlocks are lessons, and the drawer is the codex. The Gym Teacher (below) is the next boss to build so that a School run can end.

**Upgrades.** **Structure.** Three kinds, as now: weapons (fire something), controls (fire something that does no damage), passives (change the player). Plus **evolutions**: a weapon at max level held together with a named passive at level 3+ is offered, once, as a single card that replaces both — the genre's best mechanic and this game's best joke slot, because an evolution is *what you became*. Budget: retire the number thirty and keep the gate. Every item still states `enables`/`tradesAway` and the test still enforces it; the count is roughly seven per act (the pool is shared across acts within a run, so by the Office there are twenty-plus in play), and evolutions come from combinations rather than the budget.

**G-014 amended, not retired.** Every item still closes a door — a build is a shape and needs an outside. But the door can be *shape* (Wake covers only behind you), *timing* (Capacitation is worthless for two minutes) or *aggro* (Reply All, below), not only a stat penalty. Four of the seven Conception items already subtract by shape; the two that subtract by number (Midpiece, Membrane) read as a wince on the card, and the owner asked for "cool upgrades". Cards keep one line (G-034) and the joke carries the cost.

**What makes two builds feel different** is answered by the sim already: `seeking`/`line`/`burst`/`trail`/`attractor` are five different relationships to the crowd, and the passives change what the player *is* (fast and fragile, slow and thick, bigger). The additions below each add one relationship the sim does not have yet — chain, aggro, slow-field, hitbox-as-stat, persistence — and each is a small field on an existing mode rather than a system.

**Five that could ship:**

1. **Group Chat** — School weapon, `seeking` + `chain: 3`. *"Hits one, and then everyone it knows."* A seeking shot that on impact jumps to the three nearest enemies at falling damage. Wants a clique-dense screen and does nothing against a single hall monitor. Closes: single-target damage.

2. **Growth Spurt** — Adolescence passive. *"Taller. Longer reach. Everyone can see you."* +10% weapon range and pickup radius per level; +12% player collision radius per level. `PLAYER_RADIUS` becomes a stat. Closes: threading gaps in a crowd.

3. **Snooze** — Adolescence control, `attractor`-shaped area with `slow: 0.5` instead of `pull`. *"Nine more minutes. Everything nearby also waits."* Drops a zone where everything inside — enemies, projectiles, and the player — moves at half speed for nine seconds. Wake and Acrosome love it; Motility and Midpiece cannot use it. Closes: escaping.

4. **Reply All** — Office weapon, `burst` with `range: 9999` and `aggro: 3`. *"Reaches everyone. Nobody asked."* Every twelve seconds, 1 damage to every enemy on screen, and every `drift`/`cross` enemy becomes `chase` for three seconds. The cost is built into the mechanic: it wakes the room. Closes: being ignored, which is the only thing that has kept the player alive since act one.

5. **Legacy** — evolution, Wake max + Capacitation 3. *"Everything you left behind keeps working after you stop."* Footprints no longer expire for the rest of the act; the field slowly becomes the player's. Replaces both items. The thesis, as a card, said once.

**Enemies and bosses.** **Philosophy, unchanged and sharpened.** Enemies are conditions, institutions and abstractions (D-007, absolute); they do not react to the player (law 8) and they are roles, not people (law 9). What the new register adds is that indifference now has to be *drawn bigger*: at 48px a deadpan face was two invisible pixels, so the joke was in the data file and not on screen. Every enemy is one sim primitive plus one flag, so that the bots play it before anyone draws it. Every enemy answers "why this life stage" or does not ship (mechanism 2, kept).

**Acne** — Adolescence, `spawnAt: 'lead'` at lead 0, `contact: 'attach'`, invulnerable like the antibody, but the attach effect is `radius: +3px` per stack instead of drag, and — the first attach in the game that expires — it falls off after 40 seconds. *"You are more visible now. It clears up. It comes back."* Why this stage: adolescence is the first time the body itself is the thing being noticed, and it is temporary, which is worse. The antibody is a record and is permanent; acne is a phase and is not. Same mechanic, opposite half-life, and the player learns the difference by living through both.

**Group Project** — Service-or-College, a four-headed `cluster` (the shape is reserved per act, so College may have one) with `chase` movement, and a new small field `weakPoint`: all 40 HP sit in one head; hits on the other three do nothing and the sprite does not tell you which. *"Only one of them is doing anything. Find out which."* Why this stage: college is where the player first discovers that a group's output is one person's, and that finding out who costs more than doing the work. Law 9 holds — no head is a person; the cluster is the role.

**Meeting** — The Office, built from `RingState`, which the sim already has for the spermicide: a ring that spawns *around the player* at 260px, contracts to 120px over 30 seconds, and holds there. Nothing inside it can move faster than 60% speed; nothing outside can enter; it deals no damage and drops nothing when it ends. *"This could have been an email."* Why this stage: the Office is the first stage that takes the player's time without touching them. The ring is Conception's zone (spermicide) returning in a suit — G-001 delivered by one sim primitive reused.

**The Gym Teacher — School's boss, which has no design and blocks the act.** Stationary at the arena's edge, eight times player height, whistle and shorts as the character (law 9; the person carries them). It never attacks the player. Its `telegraph` is the whistle; its `attack` reverses the velocity of every `bounce` enemy on the field and spawns three more dodgeballs from its own position. It cannot be damaged while any dodgeball is alive — nobody leaves until the equipment is put away — and it takes damage only in the gap after the player clears the balls and before the next whistle. Three phases, no rage: the whistle gets faster. Mechanically it is the existing `idle → telegraph → attack` loop with the attack pointed at the act's own enemies instead of at the player, and an `invulnerableWhile` check over the enemy list. The joke: *it does not hit you; it makes everyone else hit you and calls it character.* It is G-004's principle — the boss is the structure, not a villain — delivered for the act the project needs next.

**Art register.** ## Why the current register is not a register at gameplay scale

Three measured facts, none of them in the prose yet:

1. **The outline is 1px in play.** `OUTLINE_RATIO = 2/96` gives a 2px ring on a 96px frame, which `ActScene` draws at `displaySize: 48`. Every swarm sprite in the game has a one-pixel outline at the size it is seen. The antibody (44px frame) gets 1px at generation. Law 1 says "one outline weight"; in play that weight is the thinnest one possible.
2. **The faces are sub-pixel.** Every prompt asks for "two small flat dots for eyes and one short straight line". At 96px those dots are ~3px; at 48px they are 1.5px. Law 5 ("faces on everything") is currently delivered in the data file and not on the screen. The joke engine of the whole game is invisible in the horde.
3. **The threat palette is quieter than the decoration.** In Oklab, threat-elite has chroma 0.080 and threat-boss 0.068; conception-mid is 0.081 and school-light is 0.083. The two threat colours the player learns last are less saturated than the act backgrounds' own middle tones. Contact red sits at hue 33°, between conception-deep (8°) and conception-mid (27°): the spermicide is drawn in the act's own hue family. Ranged gold (75°) is Service's entire palette (86–91°). Law 6 says colour carries threat; the palette values make that impossible in three of four acts.

D-018 diagnosed the register meeting the genre and responded by subtracting the register from the swarm. What is left on 90% of the screen is a muted palette, a hand-cut clause, and a 1px warm line — not a style, an absence of one. Meanwhile the one thing D-017 got right stands: two of the three "toony" signals in the first batch were the pipeline's (pure-black contour, saturated twenty-colour palette). Any register below inherits a warm ink and a controlled palette, so "too toony" cannot recur by the same route.

## Three candidates

### A. The Classroom Reel — the institutional cartoon

**References:** *Duck and Cover* (Archer Productions for the Federal Civil Defense Administration, 1951 — Bert the Turtle); the Bell System Science Series, specifically *Hemo the Magnificent* (Frank Capra, 1957), which drew blood cells as characters for schoolchildren; UPA's *Gerald McBoing-Boing* (1950) and the Mr. Magoo shorts. All three are institutions explaining your life to you in cartoon form, which is G-008's joke exactly — the game is the artifact, not a comment on it — made in the half of the period that moved.

**Why it reads at 48px in a horde of 300:** UPA/Hanna-Barbera design was born from cheapness and low resolution — a 1950s television or a classroom 16mm projector. A character is two or three large shapes; the head is 50–60% of the body; the eyes are a quarter of the head. At 48px that gives 6–7px eyes, a 2px line, and a silhouette that is one shape. It is the register that was already solving this problem in 1951.

**How it is funny:** Bert the Turtle is calm. The narrator is calm. A white cell drawn the way the Bell film drew it — friendly, labelled, doing its job — while it engulfs you is law 8 (indifference) as a period style rather than as a prompt clause. The substitute teacher is the film's presenter figure, the one who points at the chart. The Egg is the film's final diagram. Nothing on screen is mean; everything on screen is being helpful.

**The eleven laws:** 1 keep, amend `OUTLINE_RATIO` to 4/96 (2px in play; the ink stays warm, D-017's diagnosis was blackness not weight). 2 keep — cel paint is flat by definition; grain (texture.ts) stays as film grain. 3 keep the mechanism (quantise, check), **re-cut the values** (below). 4 keep — UPA is hand-drawn and wobbly; the Reorg stays the only ruled thing (G-013) and the antibody keeps its ruled Y. 5 keep and **enforce**: a new check `readable-48px-face` counts ink-coloured connected components inside the silhouette at 48px and requires two of at least 3px — the first time law 5 will have a test. 6 keep. 7 keep, every check stays. 8 keep — this register is indifference personified. 9 keep — the presenter figure is a role by construction. 10 keep, extend: **chroma has one job** — threat colours are the only high-chroma colours on the field; act tones and neutrals stay under a chroma ceiling; enforced in the palette test. 11 keep. D-018's detail budget: **retire** — a register born at 48px needs no two-tier surface; bosses get real frames (D-006) instead of halftone.

**Prompt suffix** (replaces `SHARED_STYLE[0..1]` and `DETAIL_CLAUSE.small`; the chroma-background, no-shadow, no-text, no-frame clauses stay; the "not cute, not childish, not a modern cartoon" clause is dropped):

```
1950s limited-animation cartoon cel, in the manner of a mid-century classroom educational film or civil-defence short, bold simplified character design built from two or three large shapes, a large head and big flat oval eyes, thick even ink outline in a warm near-black, flat cel-paint colours in a limited palette, saturated but slightly dulled like faded film stock, large uninterrupted areas of flat colour, strong clear silhouette readable at a distance, the face large and plainly visible, no halftone dots, no hatching, no surface texture, no gradients, no rendered lighting, no shading, strictly flat two-dimensional, calm and deadpan, indifferent, looking somewhere else
```

### B. Signage — the pictogram field

**References:** ISO 7010 safety pictograms and the wet-floor/exit-man idiom; Otl Aicher's 1972 Munich Olympic pictograms; Microsoft Office 97 clip art and the Wingdings man. This is G-037's `pictogram` clause — already in `batch.ts` for the icons — promoted from the cards to the field.

**Why it reads at 48px:** it is the only tradition whose entire discipline is legibility at a distance. No interior line at all; identity is silhouette, literally (law 6 made total). Pictograms are the thing the CHECK stage is measuring for.

**How it is funny:** nothing on screen is a person; your life is wayfinding. The threat colours get real semantics for free — contact is prohibition red, ranged is warning yellow, elite is mandatory blue, and boss is the green of a "safe condition" sign, which makes the Egg the EXIT sign, and being let in is leaving by the marked route. Homework's reserved wedge becomes a hazard triangle with a face. And law 4 inverts into a better joke than the Reorg's: **the player is the only thing on screen drawn by a hand** — everything else is signage, and the one wobbly figure with a cowlick is the one person in the building.

**The eleven laws:** 1 **retire** — a pictogram has no outline; the shape is the mark (1px separation ring at most). 2 keep. 3 keep mechanism, re-cut to the ISO palette. 4 **invert**: mechanical, never hand-cut — except the player. G-013 loses its exception (the Reorg is no longer special). 5 keep, with the face as two solid dots and a bar, which pictograms can hold. 6 keep, now literal. 7 keep. 8 keep — a sign is the least reactive thing there is. 9 keep, free. 10 keep, and the semantics improve. 11 keep. D-018 retire. It is the most legible option and the coldest; it risks the later acts reading as a hazard-sign catalogue rather than a life.

**Prompt suffix:**

```
a flat pictogram in the manner of international safety and wayfinding signage, one solid silhouette with no outline, geometrically constructed from circles, straight bars and rounded caps, mechanically drafted, maximum simplification, every feature a solid blob or bar, designed to be recognised across a room, two flat colours plus the face marks, the face as two solid dots and one bar and clearly visible, no interior lines, no texture, no gradients, no shading, strictly flat, calm and deadpan, indifferent, facing off to one side
```

### C. The Toy Aisle — 1960s board-game box art

**References:** Milton Bradley's *The Game of Life* box (1960) — a life as a board with pegs in cars; Hasbro's *Operation* (1965), Cavity Sam — a diagram of a body with a face; *Mouse Trap* (1963). Thick-line toy-catalogue cartooning in plastic primaries.

**Why it reads at 48px:** box art was drawn to read across a toy aisle; chunky rounded proportions, no fine line. **Pipeline risk is real:** the generator will draw airbrushed plastic highlights, CONFORM will quantise the gradients to speckle, and `palette-conformance` will fail or, worse, pass over a mottled sprite. Law 2 has to be fought for on every asset.

**How it is funny:** you bought this life at Sears. It is warm and nostalgic, which is its danger — the register is cosy where the game is bleak, and Operation's patient is *anxious*, which fights law 8 on every human figure.

**The eleven laws:** 1 keep at 4/96. 2 keep and enforce hardest — add a "no highlight" check (no second light tone inside a fill). 3 re-cut to plastic primaries. 4 keep, chunky. 5 keep, enforce. 6 keep. 7 keep. 8 **at risk**. 9 keep. 10 keep. 11 keep.

**Prompt suffix:**

```
1960s board-game box illustration, chunky thick-line cartoon in the manner of vintage toy-catalogue art, rounded simplified toy-like forms, bright flat plastic colours, a large plainly visible face, thick warm near-black outline, flat colour fills, no airbrush, no highlights, no gloss, no shine, no gradients, no shading, strictly flat two-dimensional, calm and deadpan, indifferent
```

## One Life, One Build — Busytown on an insurance form  
*angle: propose:systems-designer*

**Thesis.** Build identity in this game should come from the one thing no other survivors-like can offer: your items are habits and circumstances that persist for the whole life and change shape only at the act transitions, so what you did at School is literally what you do at the Office. The current rules (seven subtractive items, a 30-item cap, a register that is illegible at sprite scale) were deliberate but shrink the possibility space; slot caps, cross-act evolutions and a cartoon field on pamphlet paper widen it while keeping every hard constraint.

**The run.** One run is one life, about 30 minutes: seven acts of roughly four minutes plus a boss (Conception stays at 300s until a person has played it, per D-022). Each act is a self-contained horde with its own five enemies and its own accumulator (antibody, homework, group chat, email, bills, stairs, knees; ITEMS-BRIEF §3.3 answered yes, the irreversible-drag enemy is the spine in costume, one per act). Items never reset: the four habits and four circumstances you own walk into the next act with their levels, and the act transition is the only moment anything changes shape (an evolution) — mid-act you never become anyone else, which is true of lives. The Egg's unchosen inheritance (G-017) is finally implemented because act two exists to carry it. A labelled fraction of the accumulator carries across the transition (placeholder 50%, provisional sentence in the data), so Decline feels like decline; play decides the number.

Losing: you die of something that is not natural causes, and the game issues an obituary in the certificate's layout: "Cause of death: Dodgeball. Age: 9. Survived by: Reputation (4), Braces." The sim already knows the killer; it needs a `killedBy` field. Winning: Decline's boss is Time, which cannot be damaged; survive its last ninety seconds and you die of natural causes and get the certificate (G-002 unchanged).

Between runs: the family tree. Every finished life, won or lost, is a row, and the next life may start with one Circumstance of birth unlocked by what happened before — all conditions and objects, never categories of person (D-007): "Hand-me-downs" (start with the previous life's highest-level habit at level 1; unlocked by any life reaching School), "Born early" (start with Youth 1 and 90 max HP; unlocked by dying in Conception three times), "Family business" (Office items may appear from School on; unlocked by reaching the Office), "Good bones" (accumulator carry halved; unlocked by reaching Decline). Later-act items enter the pool only once a life has reached that act, which is the standard unlock and keeps the first lives small.

**Upgrades.** THE SHAPE. Two kinds, in life words: habits (what you do; every existing weapon and control) and circumstances (what happened to you; every existing passive). Four slots of each. Six levels per item. A fourth card on every offer, "Not now" (skip, +10% health, keeps the slot) — ITEMS-BRIEF §3.4's loaded option, no longer a trap because slots make declining rational. Evolutions: a habit at max level plus its paired circumstance at level 1 or more becomes its next form at the next act transition, announced on the act card ("Raised Hand — was Lash"), never in a menu. An evolved form gets a fresh three-level track. An item that never evolves keeps its name with a tag in small type: "Lash · since Conception". That tag is the theme delivered by UI, which is the only channel G-001 permits, and it is funny at the Office.

THE BUDGET, and why. Bots today: 8.5–22.5 picks in a five-minute act, median 13 for the winning policy. With cumulative XP the later acts yield fewer, so a life is roughly 60–75 picks. Capacity must roughly match: 8 slots × 6 levels = 48, plus up to eight evolutions at 3 = 72. Pool: five new items per act (3 habits, 2 circumstances; Conception keeps its 4+3+1), 36 by Decline, plus evolutions which are combinations rather than pool entries. A life is a choice of 8 from 36 and two lives differ in kind, not magnitude. Mechanism 5's gate stays (every item states the build it enables); its number was set before persistence was on the table and is restated, not removed. G-014 is retired as a rule and kept as taste: cost it where the cost is the joke (Youth, Braces), and "a slot" is a legal `tradesAway`.

WHAT MAKES TWO BUILDS DIFFERENT. Slots (you cannot be everything), pairs (Youth is the key to both Raised Hand and Popularity, so maxing one is not maxing the other), lineage (a Reputation life and a Gossip life look nothing alike by the Office), and the accumulator (Charisma gathers the unkillable thing in every act, G-023 generalised).
